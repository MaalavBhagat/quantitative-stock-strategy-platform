# Results summary — does "sell after a small rise, rebuy after a dip" beat buy-and-hold?

> **SIMULATED, HISTORICAL results. Not a prediction and not financial advice.** Every number below is copied from a
> report generated by code from saved run records; the source is given in brackets.
> [V] = reports/experiments/validation_2017-2020.md · [H] = reports/experiments/validation_headline_2017-2020.md ·
> [P5] = reports/experiments/phase5_comparison_dev_2010-2016.md.

## 1. The question and the answer
**Question (H1 v2):** starting with AUD 10,000 in one US large-cap stock, does a rule that sells after a rise of X%
and buys back after a fall of Y% (X, Y = 2–5%) end with more money than simply buying and holding the same stock with
dividends reinvested — after brokerage, currency conversion and spread, on years not used to choose the settings?

**Answer: no. REJECTED** on the 2017–2020 validation period, under rules written down and tagged in git *before* any
2017–2020 data existed (`prereg-v1`, `prereg-v1.1`; research/PREREGISTRATION.md). The four variations of the idea
(pullback, mean reversion, two trend-filtered versions) were **also REJECTED** [V §1].

## 2. What was tested
5 strategies (S1 fixed profit target, S2 pullback from peak, S3 mean reversion, S4-S1 and S4-S2 = S1/S2 with a trend
filter) against S0 buy-and-hold · 70 settings per stock · 8 stocks (AAPL, MSFT, KO, PG, XOM, INTC, T, SPY) ·
3,976 validation runs · 7 cost/spread variants (Stake as the main case; tight / wide spread; IBKR; CommSec; whole
shares; wide spread + CommSec) [V header, §4].

## 3. Headline results (validation 2017–2020, main settings) [H §H1]
| Strategy | Verdict | Stocks where it beat buy-and-hold (of 8) | Median result vs buy-and-hold | Runs stuck in cash at the end | Median days fully in cash |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 fixed profit target | REJECTED | 0 | −43.9 points | 96/128 (75%) | 89% |
| S2 pullback from peak | REJECTED | 0 | −12.1 points | 47/128 (37%) | 30% |
| S3 mean reversion | REJECTED | 3 | −19.4 points | 38/48 (79%) | 56% |
| S4-S1 trend filter + S1 | REJECTED | 1 | −32.2 points | 93/128 (73%) | 53% |
| S4-S2 trend filter + S2 | REJECTED | 1 | −2.3 points | 7/128 (5%) | 6% |

"Points" = the strategy's total return minus buy-and-hold's total return, in percentage points (USD). A strategy
"beats buy-and-hold on a stock" when its middle (median) setting does.

## 4. Why it failed (from the evidence)
- **Selling early and waiting for a dip gets you stranded when prices keep rising.** S1 ended stuck in cash in 96 of
  128 runs; on AAPL it was fully in cash on 99% of days and on MSFT 98% [H §H1; V §2]. Most of the 2017–2020 years
  were rising years, so waiting for a dip usually meant missing the rise.
- **The development-period "winners" did not last.** S4-S2 on AAPL beat buy-and-hold in 14 of 16 settings in
  2010–2016 but only 2 of 16 in 2017–2020 (1 of the 14 kept winning). S1 and S4-S1 on XOM beat it in 4 and 7 settings
  in 2010–2016, and in 0 of 16 in 2017–2020. One exception, reported honestly: S3 on XOM went from 1 of 6 to 5 of 6 [V §6; P5 §2].
- **Gains rest on a few trades.** In runs that made money, the top 3 round trips gave a median 96% of the gain for S1,
  90% for S4-S1 and 83% for S3 (S2 34%, S4-S2 55%) [V §6]. A result that depends on a few lucky trades is fragile.
- **Costs hurt the strategies that trade most.** With CommSec-level costs the median result falls from −2.3 to −15.1
  points for S4-S2, from −12.1 to −29.0 for S2 and from −19.4 to −31.5 for S3; S1, which hardly trades, barely moves
  (−43.9 → −48.7). Buy-and-hold itself is almost unaffected (median 61.68% main vs 60.41% CommSec) [V §4].
- **The market type matters, and rising years dominated.** Of 32 stock-years, 18 were UP, 5 DOWN and 9 SIDEWAYS.
  S3 did better than buy-and-hold in DOWN years (median +13.7 points) but worse in UP years (−16.6); S1 lost −25.2
  points in UP years [V §5]. The rules mostly help when prices fall — and prices mostly rose.

## 5. What this evidence does NOT show (limits)
- **Survivorship:** all 8 stocks are companies that still exist; free data covers few failed companies
  (DATA_SOURCES.md §8). Results for "buy the dip" on stocks that later collapsed could be worse.
- **Only 8 US large-cap stocks/ETF and one 4-year validation period.** Other stocks, markets or years may differ.
- **Daily prices only** — decisions at the close, trades at the next open; no intraday detail.
- **No tax** (capital gains tax ignored; real life favours buy-and-hold more — EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md limits table), **assumed spreads**, **one main data source**
  (Tiingo, cross-checked with Yahoo), and one known bad price bar (INTC 2014-06-13, development period only).
- The final test period (2021+) has **not** been run (see research/FINAL_TEST_OPTIONS.md).

## 6. What this means for a small Australian investor (facts only — not financial advice)
- Costs used (research/BROKER_FEES.md): Stake US$3 per trade and 0.55% currency conversion when funding and
  withdrawing; CommSec 0.55% currency conversion on **every** trade plus brokerage. Frequent trading pays these again
  and again; buy-and-hold pays them about once.
- The currency moves too. AUD/USD went from 0.724 (2017-01-04) to 0.7702 (2020-12-31), so the same holdings were worth
  less in AUD: buy-and-hold on SPY returned 76.13% in USD but 63.63% in AUD [H §H2].
- This project tested past data only. It is **not financial advice** and says nothing certain about the future.

## 7. Questions with no pre-registered verdict (descriptive only — not judged)
- **H2 (better risk-adjusted result):** not judged. Descriptive: maximum drawdown stayed within 5 points of
  buy-and-hold on 8 of 8 stocks for S1, S2, S3 and S4-S1, and 7 of 8 for S4-S2 [V §1, R3].
- **H5 (works in sideways/volatile markets):** not judged. Descriptive: S3 +13.7 points in DOWN years, +0.6 in
  SIDEWAYS, −16.6 in UP years [V §5].
- **H6 (depends on stock type):** not judged. Descriptive: S3's median result was positive on XOM (+16.9), T (+25.2)
  and INTC (+3.6), and negative on AAPL, MSFT, KO, PG and SPY [V §2]. (S0 on XOM −46.32%, T −18.59%,
  INTC +47.22% [H §H2].)
